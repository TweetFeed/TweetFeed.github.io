Frequently asked questions

What is an AI campaign on TweetFeed?

A campaign groups multiple IOCs reported by the community that AI detects as belonging to the same underlying operation - shared infrastructure, malware family, or targeted brand - seen over the last 30 days. The grouping itself, the name, the context narrative, the confidence score and the apparent target are AI-inferred; the counts, dates, IOC types, anchors and the raw IOCs underneath are derived deterministically from the feed.

How is campaign confidence calculated?

Confidence (high, medium, low) is the AI's assessment of how cohesive the cluster is - how strongly the underlying IOCs appear related, not a maliciousness score or a verdict on any single indicator. Shared registrable domains, matching tags and overlapping reporters raise it; loosely related indicators lower it. Treat low confidence as a lead to verify, not a verdict.

How often is the campaign data refreshed?

Campaigns are recomputed daily from a rolling 30-day window of TweetFeed IOCs. This page shows campaigns still active this week by default - switch to 'All (30 days)' to see the full window. If a generation run fails, this page shows a stale-data warning and falls back to the last successful snapshot.

Where does the underlying IOC data come from?

The same source as the rest of TweetFeed: indicators of compromise shared publicly by infosec researchers on Twitter/X, deduplicated and published every 15 minutes. Campaign clustering is an AI layer on top of that same feed.
